Cutaway

Six layers. You only ever see one.

This is a shower wall in cross-section. The tile is the last quarter-inch. Everything behind it is what decides whether the job lasts twenty years or leaks in three — and it is exactly where a cheap bid cuts.

  1. Framing & blocking

    Studs straight and plumb, with solid blocking set where a grab bar or bench will land later.

  2. Cement backer board

    Never drywall. Screwed off to spec with the seams taped in mesh and mortar.

  3. Waterproof membrane

    The layer that actually keeps water in the shower. Corners and the pan seam get extra attention.

  4. Thinset mortar

    Notched and back-buttered for full coverage — hollow spots are where tile cracks later.

  5. Tile

    Laid off a level line, not off the floor, so the joints stay true all the way up.

  6. Sealed grout

    Cured, then sealed. The only layer you will ever see, and the last one we touch.

Questions

Answers before you call

The things people ask us most often, up front.

Do you charge for estimates?
No. We come out, walk the space with you and give you a written, line-item estimate at no cost. If the project needs design drawings or engineering, we will tell you that up front and what it costs before anything is ordered.
Are you licensed and insured?
Yes — licensed, insured and carrying liability coverage. We are glad to send you a copy of the certificate before work starts, and you should ask any contractor you are considering for the same thing.
How long will my project take?
A drywall repair and repaint is usually a few days. A bathroom typically runs two to four weeks; a kitchen four to eight; an addition several months once permits are issued. Your estimate includes a real start and finish date, and we tell you immediately if a material lead time moves it.
Can I stay in the house during the work?
Almost always, yes. We phase the work so you keep a functioning kitchen or bathroom for as long as possible, seal off the work area to keep dust out of the rest of the house, and clean up at the end of each day.
Do you handle permits?
Yes. For additions, structural changes and most remodels we pull the permits and meet the inspectors ourselves. Permit fees are listed as their own line on your estimate so you can see exactly what they cost.
What happens if something comes up mid-project?
Older homes hide things — rot, old wiring, a wall that turns out to be load-bearing. When we find something, we stop, show you, and give you a written change order with the price before we do the work. Nothing gets added to your invoice that you have not approved.
How do payments work?
A deposit to schedule and order materials, then progress payments tied to milestones you can actually see completed, and a final payment after the walkthrough and punch list are done. The full schedule is written into your estimate before you sign anything.
